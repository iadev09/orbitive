# Transport baseline, 12 November 2026

orbit-link 0.6.7 in preparation, release profile, 64-byte requests, 5 seconds
per row, three runs. See [how these are measured](README.md). This is the
record later changes to the inbox bell are compared with: the same command on
the same machine, before and after.

```sh
cargo bench -p orbit-link --bench transport --features tokio,probe -- \
  --sizes 64 --conns 1,8,64 --seconds 5
```

## What it shows

A call that fits its frames sleeps as often over Link as over a connected
socket. With one call at a time both park twice per exchange, and Link rings
two bells; at 8 outstanding a quarter of a bell and a quarter of a park per
exchange; at 64 almost none. The bell is rung only when the far thread sleeps.

What differs is the price of a wake. One bell send takes about 2.5
microseconds here, where the socket's whole write takes 0.375, and the bell is
read with calls that find nothing: six per exchange with one call at a time.

## What it leaves out

Linux rows are not recorded yet. macOS does not count voluntary context
switches, so that column is zero here; parks are counted by the runtime.

## macOS

macOS 27.2, Apple M3 Max, not pinned, with ordinary desktop activity. One wake
between two threads costs 2.42 microseconds on this machine
(`cargo bench -p orbit-core --bench wake`). The stamps move in steps of about
42 ns.
