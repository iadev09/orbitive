# Transport after the bell changes, 12 November 2026

orbit-link 0.6.7 in preparation, release profile, 64-byte requests, 5 seconds
per row, three runs on each machine. Compared with
[the baseline](transport-2026-11-12.md): the same command on the same machine.
See [how these are measured](README.md).

Two changes to the inbox bell, measured one after the other:

1. **Exact reads.** The receiver reads one bell for each arm a writer took,
   instead of reading the socket until it says it is empty.
2. **Connected bell.** A writer rings a lane over a socket connected to that
   lane's bell once, instead of naming the lane for every ring.

## What they show

A call that fits its frames is cheaper at every load on both machines, and
nothing else changed: the socket and the held stream are where they were.

| `frame`, 64 bytes | Linux before | Linux after | macOS before | macOS after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| one call, p50 | 17.1 to 17.4 µs | 14.0 to 14.3 µs | 12.6 to 13.6 µs | 9.3 to 9.9 µs |
| one call, CPU per exchange | 19.4 to 19.6 µs | 16.1 to 16.3 µs | 13.1 to 14.1 µs | 10.0 to 10.3 µs |
| 8 outstanding, ops/s | 326 to 336 thousand | 362 to 375 thousand | 392 to 441 thousand | 670 to 677 thousand |
| 64 outstanding, ops/s | 806 to 808 thousand | 839 to 874 thousand | 1.38 to 1.47 million | 2.13 to 2.29 million |
| empty bell reads per lone exchange | 6.00 | 0.00 | 6.00 | 0.00 |
| one bell send, one call | 2.09 to 2.18 µs | 2.07 to 2.15 µs | 2.53 to 2.60 µs | 0.84 to 0.93 µs |

The two machines disagree about the second change. On macOS a bell send fell
to a third: there, naming the lane was most of its cost. On Linux a bell send
with one call at a time costs what it did, about 2.1 microseconds against 1.35
for a connected socket's whole write. A lone exchange is still about a
microsecond shorter there after the change, so that gain lies outside the send
itself, and under load a send is somewhat cheaper (1.7 to 1.8 microseconds at
64 outstanding against 2.1). What a bell that wakes a sleeper costs on Linux
beyond a stream socket's write is not explained by these rows.

With one call at a time a connected Unix socket is still ahead on both
machines: 8.2 to 8.6 microseconds on Linux, 3.6 to 4.0 on macOS.

## What changed besides

Reading the bell exactly also removed an unintended short look before parking:
a frame that arrived during the removed calls was caught without a sleep. The
receiver now reaches the park sooner. Parks per exchange rose slightly under
load (`frame` at 64 outstanding: 0.03 to 0.04 on Linux, 0.03 to 0.05 on macOS).
On Linux the `link` path at 8 outstanding rings and parks more often (0.28 to
0.30 bells per exchange before, 0.45 to 0.46 after) and is faster all the same
(265 to 276 thousand ops/s before, 299 to 308 thousand after).

A writer now keeps one descriptor for each lane it has rung.

The wake floor of the Linux machine differed between the two measurements,
taken hours apart (1.33 microseconds per wake at the baseline, 0.90 at this
one). The socket and the held stream, which these changes do not touch,
measured the same both times.
