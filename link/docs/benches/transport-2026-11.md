# Transport results, November 2026

orbit-link 0.5.8, release profile, 5 seconds per row. See
[how these are measured](README.md).

## What they show

Under load with small messages Orbit leads, because a receiver that is already
awake needs no wake and the sender makes no system call.

| 64 bytes, 64 calls outstanding | `uds` ops/s | `stream` ops/s | `link` ops/s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Linux | 319,196 | 1,310,097 (4.1x) | 516,390 (1.6x) |
| macOS | 837,796 | 2,827,729 (3.4x) | 1,305,510 (1.6x) |

CPU per operation there is lower too: 1,480 and 3,795 ns against 6,217 on Linux,
671 and 1,463 ns against 2,382 on macOS.

With one call at a time a Unix socket is faster, on both systems. The other side
is asleep for every exchange, and an Orbit wake is itself a Unix datagram, sent
beside the data rather than with it.

| 64 bytes, one call at a time | `uds` p50 | `stream` p50 | `link` p50 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Linux | 8,318 ns | 14,878 ns | 19,114 ns |
| macOS | 3,958 ns | 11,000 ns | 14,709 ns |

Large bodies depend on the system. At 128 KB macOS favours Orbit at every
concurrency here, and Linux favours the socket with one call at a time.

| 128 KB | `uds` ops/s | `stream` ops/s | `link` ops/s |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Linux, 1 call | 20,724 | 10,332 | 10,394 |
| Linux, 64 calls | 18,348 | 22,291 | 21,445 |
| macOS, 1 call | 4,405 | 16,969 | 16,057 |
| macOS, 64 calls | 33,818 | 109,396 | 72,863 |

## What they leave out

Round-trip time measures the bytes. A link segment also gives the processes shared
state: queue depth, who is alive and which name serves which lane can be read
without asking, and there are no connections to pool. None of that appears here.
